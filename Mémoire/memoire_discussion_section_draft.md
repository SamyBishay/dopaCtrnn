## 4.2 Limitations of the current model

The results reported in §3 are interpreted within the framework of a specific, intentionally minimal model. Several design constraints limit the generality of the conclusions and should be stated explicitly.

**Task simplicity.** The DNMTP T-maze imposes a single binary working-memory item — which of two arms was visited — over a fixed delay. This is a deliberately minimal test of the Villet phenomenon: it asks whether the mechanisms are sufficient, not whether they are general. The model is not tested on tasks requiring multiple concurrent WM items, variable reward magnitudes, hierarchical action sequences, or non-spatial rule structures. It is therefore possible that the four design choices are sufficient only for this specific task structure, and that richer demands would require architectural extensions — most obviously a richer observation space or a higher-capacity habitual system. Claims about the model's generality should be read accordingly.

**The observational asymmetry is an imposed assumption, not a learned outcome.** The allocentric/egocentric split assigns spatial coordinates to the goal-directed system and withholds them from the habitual system by construction. This is biologically motivated — dorsolateral striatal circuits respond to stimulus-response contingencies rather than allocentric maps (Packard & McGaugh, 1996 [TODO: verify]) — but the present work does not test whether the split is *necessary* for the handoff, or whether a network with symmetric observation access would spontaneously learn to use spatial information in only one pathway. That question is explicitly deferred (§4.3.1). Similarly, the low-rank constraint on the habitual recurrent weights is a structural prior, not an emergent consequence of the learning rule; whether low-rank representations would arise spontaneously from APE training with unconstrained weights is not tested here.

**The DA expression gain is a proximal implementation of a more complex mechanism.** The present model implements dopamine as a readout-level multiplicative gain on goal-directed output activations (`r_out = gain(DA) · tanh(h)`), corresponding to the excitability/expression aspect of Naudé et al.'s (2024) dual-mechanism account. Naudé et al. demonstrate that dopamine's build function — the slow synaptic plasticity that constructs latent attractor basins — is mechanistically separable from the reveal function used here. The model therefore does not test whether attractor construction is necessary for the handoff, whether a purely excitability-based mechanism can produce attractors deep enough to support the 40-step delay, or whether recurrent-gain modulation (`W_eff = f(DA) · W` inside the dynamics) would produce different geometry from the readout-gain approximation used. These are testable distinctions that the present design collapses.

**The habitual learning signal approximates biological APE.** The habitual system is trained by a per-step KL divergence of its policy against the combined policy — a soft behavioural cloning target that is value-free by construction. This approximates the tail-of-striatum action prediction error documented by Greenstreet et al. (2025), but differs from the biological signal in at least two respects: the biological APE is movement-locked and arises from a dopaminergic source anatomically separate from the RPE system, whereas the present approximation uses a loss computed from the learned combined policy. Whether these differences affect the structural devaluation insensitivity or the stability of the habitual system under richer task demands is not determined by the present results.

**The DA signal is a scalar with no spatial structure.** Real dopamine is spatiotemporally heterogeneous: release propagates as directional waves across the dorsal striatum with DMS-first dynamics on instrumental trials (Hamid et al., 2021), and VTA and substantia nigra compacta project distinct signals to functionally segregated striatal territories (Engel et al., 2024). The model's single scalar DA-request conflates these sources and cannot account for the graded, region-specific credit assignment that the wave dynamics implement. In the one-bit T-maze this simplification is unlikely to be costly, but in tasks with richer spatial structure or longer temporal dependencies it may misattribute the mechanisms driving the handoff.

**The widen/deepen subpopulation structure is present only in the goal-directed system.** The habitual system has uniform time constants and no fast/slow subpopulation split. Whether the delay-period working-memory representation documented in H6 would benefit from a similar timescale separation in the habitual network — or whether the low-rank constraint is sufficient to produce stable attractor geometry without it — is not tested. Likewise, the widen/deepen time constants in the goal-directed system are fixed hyperparameters rather than emergent from the learning dynamics; whether the same τ_fast/τ_slow split would arise under direct optimisation is an open question.

**H5 and the evaluation confound.** The reactivation probe (H5) requires stochastic action sampling at evaluation. Under greedy (argmax) decoding, silencing the habitual system would cause the combined policy to collapse to `argmax(w_GD · π_GD)`, making performance recovery a mathematical identity of the decoding rule rather than evidence for a dormant-but-intact goal-directed solution. All H5 results reported in §3 were obtained under stochastic evaluation; if the stochastic-eval rerun was not completed before submission, H5 is not conclusively tested and this is stated explicitly (§3, H5). The evaluation confound is noted here as a methodological limitation of the earlier analysis pipeline that has been corrected for the present report.

## 4.3 Future research

The current model is a minimal implementation of one specific claim: that dopamine as an expression gain on intact weights, combined with value-free habitual learning and an observational asymmetry, is sufficient to reproduce the Villet reversible-handoff pattern. What the model makes possible is a sequence of experiments that test progressively more mechanistic claims, each with a clear prediction that follows directly from the present architecture. The following programme is proposed in approximate order of proximity to the current design.

### 4.3.1 Learning the observational asymmetry

The ego/allo split is currently an inductive bias. The most direct question it raises is whether a dual-system network with symmetric, full observation access would spontaneously partition spatial information — learning to represent allocentric coordinates in the goal-directed pathway and to ignore them in the habitual pathway — without any architectural imposition. Moskovitz et al. (2024) show, using a minimum-description-length argument (MDL-C), that multi-task agents can learn efficient input compression that resembles this kind of specialisation. Testing this on the T-maze would require removing the obs_dim split, giving both systems access to the full 6D observation, and measuring whether the habitual system's learned representations become position-invariant over training. The prediction from the present model is that, without the architectural split, the habitual system will acquire some sensitivity to spatial position, weakening the devaluation dissociation (H3) — because a spatially-informed system can, in principle, revise its strategy when reward contingencies change. If the split *is* learned spontaneously, it would constitute evidence that the asymmetry is not a modelling assumption but an emergent property of the dual-system optimisation problem.

### 4.3.2 Dopamine and effective integration timescales (H_tau)

The present model tests DA as a gain on output activations. A natural mechanistic extension is to test whether DA modulation of integration timescales — not just expression — changes the handoff dynamics and the working-memory geometry. The hypothesis (H_tau) is that DA recruitment shortens effective timescales in the widen subpopulation (accelerating convergence to a decision attractor) and lengthens them in the deepen subpopulation (stabilising the WM attractor across the delay). This predicts that under high DA demand (learning phase), widen units should show fast, transient trajectories converging rapidly to decision states, while deepen units should show persistent, slowly decaying trajectories. Under low DA demand (maintenance phase), both effects should weaken. Testing this requires implementing DA as a time-constant modulator (`τ_eff = τ · f(DA)`) and measuring fixed-point stability, participation ratio, and convergence speed as a function of DA level and training phase.

### 4.3.3 Dopamine's build and reveal functions as separable mechanisms

The present model uses readout-gain as a proxy for both of Naudé et al.'s (2024) dopamine functions. A programmatic next step is to implement each separately and test which is sufficient for the Villet pattern. Three conditions can be compared: (a) recurrent-gain modulation only (`W_eff = f(DA) · W` inside the CTRNN dynamics, with no output gain), (b) excitability modulation only (additive DA-dependent bias term in the hidden-state update), and (c) DA-dependent Hebbian plasticity (three-factor rule modulating weight update magnitude). The prediction from Naudé et al. is that plasticity alone builds attractors that are too shallow to drive approach without excitability modulation, that excitability alone has no directional effect without a pre-existing attractor, and that only their combination produces a system capable of the instant reactivation result. Testing this hierarchy would establish which mechanism is load-bearing for the reversible handoff and which is merely permissive.

### 4.3.4 Opponent D1/D2 plasticity in the habitual system

The habitual system currently uses a single net linear readout trained by KL-divergence APE. The OpAL* framework (Jaskir & Frank) demonstrates that opponent D1-driven (Go) and D2-driven (NoGo) pathways with nonlinear three-factor Hebbian plasticity confer normative advantages in sparse-reward environments: the D1 pathway specialises in high-reward options, the D2 pathway in low-reward options, and their combination outperforms Q-learning under reward sparsity. Replacing the habitual system's KL-APE rule with a biologically grounded three-factor Hebbian rule would test two predictions: first, whether the structural devaluation insensitivity is preserved under a more realistic learning rule (a rule that never receives a reward signal should still produce value-free associations); second, whether the low-rank constraint remains necessary or whether Hebbian nonlinearity spontaneously produces low-dimensional, stereotyped representations in the habitual pathway. Frank (2025) provides additional motivation: D2 neurons coding cost-avoidance independently of reward value suggest that the NoGo pathway may itself be the structural source of devaluation insensitivity, rather than the absence of a reward term in the loss function.

### 4.3.5 Spatiotemporal dopamine and multi-area credit assignment

The scalar DA-request signal in the present model is spatially uniform across the model. The wave-like spatiotemporal structure of dopamine release documented by Hamid et al. (2021) — with directional propagation from DMS to DLS on instrumental trials, implementing graded credit assignment by region — motivates a two-pathway DA extension. In this extension, an RPE-driven DMS-targeted signal would modulate the goal-directed system's expression gain with a short temporal lag, while an APE-driven DLS-targeted signal would modulate a habitual plasticity term with a longer lag, the two propagating with different delays and declining in the reverse order on Pavlovian trials. The key prediction is that the wave structure sharpens the lesion × phase double dissociation (H4): the cross-over from DMS-dependency to DLS-dependency should follow the wave propagation direction as training progresses, producing a spatiotemporal signature absent in a scalar-DA model. Engel et al.'s (2024) finding that VTA and SNc neurons drive anatomically and functionally segregated striatal signals further motivates separating the source of the DA signals in the model and testing whether source heterogeneity is necessary for the double dissociation or whether a single-source approximation suffices.

### 4.3.6 Extension to richer task demands

The most agnostic question is whether the four design choices scale beyond the one-bit T-maze. Three extensions are natural. First, a multi-item working-memory variant — requiring the agent to remember the order of visits across multiple delay periods within a single episode — would stress-test the low-rank constraint and the DA-request cost-minimisation mechanism independently: the habitual system's low-rank structure may be insufficient to maintain multiple simultaneous WM items, while the goal-directed DA-request should rise in proportion to task complexity. Second, varying reward magnitude across trials — so that devaluation is a continuous rather than binary manipulation — would test whether the expression-gain mechanism produces a graded devaluation sensitivity in the goal-directed system and whether the habitual system remains insensitive across the full range. Third, extending the delay beyond the 40-step ceiling used here, or introducing distractor events during the delay, would probe whether the deepen subpopulation's slow dynamics are sufficient for maintenance under interference, or whether additional mechanisms (such as active rehearsal or inhibition of competing attractors) are needed. Each of these extensions generates a concrete prediction from the present architecture and can be run within the same codebase with targeted parameter changes.
