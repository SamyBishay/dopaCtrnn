# echeveste2020

> Echeveste et al. (2020) — A recurrent E-I neural circuit optimized for sampling-based probabilistic inference exhibits canonical cortical dynamics (gamma oscillations, transient overshoots, stimulus-modulated variability) with distinct functional roles in fast inference.

## What the paper does

The authors trained a biologically constrained recurrent neural network (stochastic stabilized supralinear network, SSN) to perform sampling-based inference on a visual task (Gaussian scale mixture model of natural image patches). The network matched Bayesian posterior distributions up to second-order statistics (means and covariances) over a fixed stimulus set, then generalized to novel stimuli. The optimized circuit exhibited strong gamma oscillations (peaked at ~30 Hz, contrast-dependent), inhibition-dominated transients at stimulus onset, and stimulus-modulated noise variability — all phenomena absent in control networks trained on non-sampling objectives. Mathematical analyses linked oscillations to accelerated mixing time (sampling convergence) and transients to reduced burn-in error under continual inference, confirming experimentally in macaque V1 recordings.

## Key claims relevant to this project

- **Recurrent dynamics emerge as a functional consequence of the computational objective, not merely from architecture or optimization constraints.** Control networks with identical architecture but different training objectives (matching means only, or non-sampling-based probabilistic representations) did not exhibit gamma, transients, or variability modulation — ruling out these as architectural byproducts [Results, Control networks section].

- **Dynamical irreversibility (asymmetric E-I lags) is functionally critical to fast sampling and is absent from time-reversible algorithms (Langevin dynamics).** The optimized network showed marked asymmetry in excitatory-to-inhibitory cross-correlograms and stimulus-dependent modulation of E-I lag, faster by an order of magnitude than standard Langevin sampling [Results, Fast sampling section].

- **Gamma oscillations reduce autocorrelogram area and thus mixing time; frequency modulation (higher gamma with higher contrast) reflects adaptive allocation of oscillatory power to highest-variance directions.** Only frequencies ≥30 Hz provided measurable speedup under the network's membrane time constants [Results, Oscillations improve mixing time].

- **Transient overshoots at stimulus onset serve not as prediction-error signals but as bias-correcting terms under continual inference.** The network's overshoots scaled with the *change* in steady-state response (not absolute steady-state level), matched behavior predicted by deconvolving a step-function change with the network's averaging kernel, and this scaling structure replicated in awake macaque V1 [Results, Transients support continual inference; Experimental data analysis].

- **Stimulus-dependent noise variability and response-moment structure emerge jointly as a single functional requirement of sampling-based inference.** Networks matching only posterior means (not covariances) showed minimal variability modulation and no gamma/transients [Results, Control networks section].

## Mechanism / model details

The SSN used separate but interconnected excitatory (E) and inhibitory (I) populations with supralinear input/output functions and stimulus-independent process noise. Connectivity was structured with ring topology (angular distance-dependent coupling), reducing free parameters for stability. The network was optimized to produce response distributions matching Bayesian posteriors under a Gaussian scale mixture (GSM) generative model via a cost function penalizing divergences in means *and* covariances plus a slowness term encouraging fast dynamics.

Sampling was implemented via a Langevin-like out-of-equilibrium dynamical process. The network discovered time-irreversible dynamics (E→I directionality, lagged inhibition) that enhanced sampling speed by breaking temporal symmetry. Oscillations emerged at the population level from interactions between E and I populations (PING mechanism, confirmed by voltage-clamp ablations); their frequency was dynamically tuned (higher under higher contrast) to concentrate power in variance-dominant modes of the stationary covariance.

Transients were analyzed via single-neuron idealized models within a sampling framework: overshoots compensate for the fact that early responses still sample from the *previous* steady-state distribution; the mathematically optimal response under continual averaging (a temporal convolution) is the deconvolution of a step with the averaging kernel, which yields transients matching those observed.

## Implications for our model

**Directly relevant to the dopaCTRNN design:**

- **Functional role of noise variability:** Echeveste demonstrates that stimulus-modulated variability is not epiphenomenal but a functional requirement for *efficient* inference over multiple timescales. In the dopaCTRNN, the goal-directed system must maintain a high-dimensional working-memory attractor (sample-arm history, task phase, value) during a ~90s delay; stimulus-dependent variability in this attractor (particularly time-irreversible, oscillatory structure) may serve a similar role — keeping the posterior representation flexible enough to integrate new evidence (reward, observation) on rapid timescales while remaining stable over long delays. Stage 1 analysis should characterize whether the vanilla CTRNN develops such variability spontaneously, or whether it requires explicit optimization for inference-like objectives (Stage 5 hypothesis: dopamine acts as a multiplicative gate on such inference-ready dynamics rather than creating them de novo).

- **Attractor geometry and timescale separation via E-I asymmetry:** The irreversibility and E-I lag structure are mechanistically key to Echeveste's fast-sampling regime. In the dopaCTRNN, the D1/D2 affinity mechanism (Section 2.2 of project overview) operationalizes a similar timescale separation: D1-sensitive (phasic-dopamine) circuits should exhibit higher-frequency, more transient dynamics (rapid decision updates), while D2-sensitive (tonic-dopamine) circuits should support deeper, longer-timescale attractor structure (sustained working-memory maintenance). Echeveste's finding that gamma frequency tracks variability and that transient magnitude scales with state change suggests that the dopaCTRNN's contrast-dependent modulation of gamma (analogous to reward or task-phase signals) could be verified by checking whether D1-dominant dynamics show frequency modulation parallel to Echeveste's stimulus-contrast prediction (Stage 6 experiment).

- **Separation of learning and expression:** Echeveste's network learns its recurrent weights to match a fixed, task-defined objective (Bayesian inference); variability and dynamics follow deterministically from that optimization. The dopaCTRNN's `W_eff = f(DA) · W` architecture inverts this: weights are learned via standard RL/APE rules with dopamine absent from the loss function, and dopamine acts post-hoc on *expression*. This is a critical inversion: Echeveste shows that the *functional requirement* (probabilistic inference) must drive the learning objective for the dynamics to be efficient. If the dopaCTRNN's goal-directed system is trained by RL (which is fundamentally non-Bayesian, non-sampling) and then gated by dopamine, the dynamics it learns may not exhibit the irreversibility and variability structure Echeveste identifies as load-bearing. Stage 5's mandatory emergence test (whether the goal-directed DA-request rises when the habitual system is ablated) depends on whether the goal-directed system exhibits the kind of inference-ready variability structure that permits *graceful* suppression by dopamine rather than having that suppression feel like damage.

- **Transient overshoots as continual-inference signatures:** The dopaCTRNN's delay period is a sustained inference problem — the agent must continuously update its belief about which arm was sampled while suppressing the urge to act. Echeveste's analysis of transients suggests that at stimulus-phase transitions (sample-arm-to-delay boundary, delay-to-test boundary), the network should exhibit orientation-tuned overshoots that scale with the *magnitude of change* in the task state, not absolute state values. Stage 1 analysis should check whether the vanilla CTRNN naturally produces such transients; if not, it suggests the task may not be driving inference-like dynamics and indicates whether Task-design modifications (e.g., adding a hidden-state estimation objective) are needed to generate Echeveste-like structure.

- **Control via dimensionality and mixing time, not via external gains:** Echeveste's network achieves adaptive timescale control (contrast-dependent gamma frequency) entirely through changes in the recurrent dynamics themselves, not by multiplying inputs or outputs by external gain factors. The dopaCTRNN's dopamine-gating mechanism is an *external* multiplicative control. This is a design tradeoff: external gating is mechanistically simpler (and consistent with known dopaminergic anatomy) but may fail to produce the dynamical efficiency Echeveste identifies as emergent from *internal* recurrent optimization. Stage 5 should explicitly test whether the goal-directed system's mixing properties (autocorrelogram, oscillation frequency, transient magnitude) degrade gracefully as dopamine is suppressed, or whether suppression breaks the dynamics in ways that require retraining — the latter would suggest that dopamine-gating, while consistent with instant reactivation (Villet's requirement), comes at the cost of dynamic inefficiency that must be compensated by other design choices.

**Stages most directly constrained:**

- **Stage 1 (single network baseline):** Echeveste's analysis pipeline (fixed-point finding, autocorrelograms, participation-ratio dimensionality, E-I lag characterization) should be adapted to the dopaCTRNN's DNMTP task. If the vanilla CTRNN does *not* exhibit Echeveste-like irreversibility or variability structure, it raises the question of whether the task is structured in a way that supports inference-like dynamics, or whether the RL training objective fails to induce them. This is load-bearing for Stage 5's mechanism: the goal-directed system cannot be suppressed gracefully by dopamine if it does not first develop the kind of dynamics Echeveste identifies.

- **Stage 5 (emergent DA-mediated handoff):** The mandatory emergence-control test (habitual-system ablation) must account for whether the goal-directed system's dynamics *recover* to Echeveste-like fast-mixing, high-variability structure when dopamine rises, or whether they remain degraded. If recovery is incomplete, it suggests either (a) the dopamine-gating mechanism is overly crude and needs refinement (e.g., frequency-specific gating, E-I-population-specific gating) or (b) the learning objective itself (RL on the full task) does not induce Echeveste-like structure and requires task redesign or an auxiliary objective.

- **Stage 6 (D1/D2 analysis):** Echeveste's prediction that oscillation frequency is adaptive (higher with higher variability/contrast) directly applies to testing whether biasing toward D1-dominant (phasic) or D2-dominant (tonic) drive reshapes gamma frequency in the predicted direction. If D1-biased circuits show higher, faster-oscillating gamma (matching phasic-dopamine behavior) and D2-biased circuits show lower-frequency, more sustained gamma, it validates the pharmacological grounding of the D1/D2 affinity mechanism (Section 2.2).

## Open questions / caveats

- **Generalization of Echeveste's inference framework to RL-trained networks:** Echeveste optimized for *Bayesian* inference; the dopaCTRNN's goal-directed system trains via *reinforcement* learning. While both are optimization problems, RL does not explicitly constrain response covariances or impose sampling-based inference as an objective. It is unresolved whether RL-trained networks on the dopaCTRNN's task will exhibit Echeveste-like variability structure, irreversibility, or gamma organization. This is testable (Stage 1) but not assumed.

- **Multiple timescales via dopamine vs. via intrinsic E-I structure:** Echeveste's fast sampling emerges from the recurrent dynamics themselves (E-I asymmetry, oscillations) without any external multiplicative gating. The dopaCTRNN's D1/D2 mechanism adds external dopamine-dependent gain modulation. It is not yet clear whether these two sources of timescale control (intrinsic recurrent irreversibility and external dopamine gating) synergize or interfere; Stage 6 analysis should measure whether the characteristic frequency and mixing time of the goal-directed system change predictably as dopamine varies, or whether dopamine gating merely suppresses the expression of dynamics that were already tuned to the task.

- **Transient structure under reward uncertainty:** Echeveste's transient analysis assumes a fixed, known generative model and analyzes overshoots at stimulus onsets (discrete, exogenous events). The dopaCTRNN's delay period involves sustained inference about a *latent* state (which arm was visited) under uncertainty, with no discrete exogenous event at the output of the delay itself (the test-phase stimulus is exogenous, but the animal's own behavior is not). Whether Echeveste's prediction (overshoots scale with change in steady state) applies to self-generated state transitions or requires exogenous events is untested and may affect how Stage 1's transient analysis should be framed.

- **Rank and dimensionality in the supervisor's reference model vs. Echeveste's inference-driven variability:** The project overview notes (Section 5) that the supervisor's reference model explains automaticity via a rank constraint on the habitual system's recurrent weights, while this dopaCTRNN project invokes dopamine gating on full-rank weights. Echeveste's analysis does not directly address rank, focusing instead on dynamical properties (oscillations, transients, variability) that emerge from an inference objective. Stage 7's effective-rank analysis should test whether the habitual system's learned weights naturally collapse to low-effective-rank structure (supporting the supervisor's hypothesis) or whether they remain full-rank but are effectively suppressed by dopamine (supporting the dopaCTRNN's hypothesis), or whether both effects occur jointly (habitual system is both low-rank *and* dopamine-gated, in which case Echeveste's framework would apply to the goal-directed system but not the habitual one).
