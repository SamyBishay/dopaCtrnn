# hamid_wave-like_2021

> Hamid et al. (2021) — Dopamine does not broadcast uniformly to striatum; instead, directional spatiotemporal waves of DA axon activation propagate across DMS and DLS in task-dependent patterns, implementing graded credit assignment to functionally specialized subregions.

## What the paper does

Two-photon and widefield imaging of dopamine axons and release across the dorsal striatum in head-fixed mice (60–80% striatal surface visible). Mice performed instrumental (action-contingent reward) vs. Pavlovian (non-contingent reward) tasks signaled by escalating auditory tones. DA responses were not globally synchronized; instead, wave-like trajectories propagated across striatum at ~0.1–1 mm/s with distance-dependent spatial correlations. **Critically**, reward delivery triggered opponent-direction waves: mediolateral (ML) waves in instrumental trials (fast DMS DA, slow DLS DA) and lateromedial (LM) waves in Pavlovian trials (delayed DMS DA, fast DLS DA). Waves reversed direction within trials after unsignaled contingency reversals. ML wave directions at reward predicted next-trial running speed and correlated with anticipatory DMS DA ramps during reward pursuit.

## Key claims relevant to this project

- **Dopamine is spatiotemporally heterogeneous, not scalar.** Contrary to the global RPE hypothesis, DA concentration and release vary across striatal regions on timescales of ~100s of milliseconds, implying local, region-specific plasticity windows [widefield imaging, optic-flow quantification].
- **Wave propagation timings directly implement credit assignment.** Temporal delays induced by wave trajectories (DMS receiving DA before DLS in instrumental trials) drive asymmetric learning across striatal subregions via eligibility-trace mismatch in TD simulations [temporal-difference RL model, Fig. 4].
- **DA waves are flexibly sculpted by inferred task controllability, not by motoric or sensory factors alone.** Opponent wave directions appear after instrumental vs. Pavlovian trials despite matched velocities and sensory structure; spurious correlations between running and reward progress (high-velocity Pavlovian trials) can trigger task-inappropriate ML waves [contingency reversal task, locomotion-sensory congruence analysis].
- **Anticipatory DA ramps in DMS signal "reward responsibility"—a tag for credit allocation.** Steeper DMS ramps during reward pursuit inversely predict latency-to-peak DA at reward across task conditions; this relationship holds in contingency reversals and predicts next-trial behavioral adjustments [ramp slope vs. latency-to-peak correlation, r ≈ −0.4].
- **The mixture-of-experts (MoE) model decomposes credit assignment across three hierarchical levels**: level 1 (DMS expert: controllability, whether actions cause tone transitions); level 2 (sub-experts for distinct action-outcome contingencies, e.g., short vs. long distance runs); level 3 (sub-expert RPEs signaled by rapid DA transients at tone transitions, modulated by trial length and tone position).

## Mechanism / model details

**Hierarchy of credit assignment via DA waves and anticipatory ramps:**

The paper's central model is a three-level MoE trained on instrumental/Pavlovian classification. At level 1, a DMS-like expert accumulates evidence for agency (controllability) as ramping signals during the anticipatory epoch, proportional to the accuracy of underlying sub-expert predictions. These ramps serve dual purposes: (i) they modulate behavioral vigor (running speed) in the current trial, and (ii) critically, they **tag which subregion is responsible for the impending reward**, indicating how much credit it should receive.

At level 2, multiple sub-experts specialize for different action-outcome contingencies (e.g., short, medium, long distance runs). Each sub-expert predicts state transitions (tone changes) using semi-Markov models.

At level 3, sub-expert RPEs (sRPEs) arise when auditory tones transition at unexpected times. Because DA rewards are delayed by the spatiotemporal wave (e.g., DMS receives DA before DLS in instrumental trials), learning in later-arriving regions is "diluted" due to eligibility-trace decay, producing steeper value functions in early-receiving regions and shallow ramps in late-receiving regions—asymmetric credit assignment without an explicit routing signal.

**Operationally**: the ramp slopes during anticipation *predict the wave direction and latency at reward*, and both together *predict next-trial behavioral adjustment* (running speed). This creates a closed loop: evidence for agency → ramp → wave direction/timing → credit to appropriate subregion → behavioral adjustment.

## Implications for our model

**Three specific constraints on the dopaCTRNN design:**

1. **Scalar dopamine is insufficient; local computation is required (Stage 1–2 checkpoint).** Hamid's spatiotemporal heterogeneity implies that even if the dopaCTRNN uses a *scalar* DA signal (as currently designed), its *effects* on postsynaptic plasticity must differ across the goal-directed vs. habitual subsystems due to their structural specialization (DMS allocentric task-solving vs. DLS egocentric stimulus-response) and/or their receptor-pharmacology tuning (D1 vs. D2 affinity). The model's `W_eff = f(DA) · W` assumption may need refinement: the function *f* may not be identical across both subsystems, even if driven by a single scalar DA level. Hamid shows that the same DA *signal* (reward delivery) produces fundamentally different wave trajectories (ML vs. LM) depending on the task's instrumental structure—suggesting that the postsynaptic interpretation of DA is context-sensitive, not a fixed transfer function.

2. **Anticipatory DA ramps are a "responsibility tag" for credit assignment (Stage 5 emergence test).** In the dopaCTRNN, the goal-directed DA-request neuron is designed to be minimized during training as the habitual system becomes competent. Hamid's findings suggest a richer picture: the goal-directed system might signal (via ramp-like DA buildup) *which subregion is responsible for the current task state*, allowing the subsequent reward DA (or phasic component request) to be preferentially received by that subregion. If the dopaCTRNN's goal-directed DA-request is implemented as an anticipatory ramp that decays as competence rises, it could simultaneously implement both the responsibility-tagging function (early in training, steep ramps indicating "I am responsible for reward") and the cost-minimization function (late in training, flat ramps indicating "habitual system is responsible, I can rest"). This would make the DA-request neuron a direct neural analog of Hamid's level-1 MoE expert—a testable prediction for the attractor analysis at Stage 5.

3. **Devaluation insensitivity in the habitual system requires structural isolation from reward, not just from dopamine (Stage 4 verification).** Hamid's MoE shows that credit assignment—the selective delivery of RPE signals to responsible subregions—is implemented via spatiotemporal wave structure. The dopaCTRNN's current design isolates the habitual system's *learning rule* from reward (APE rule, not RL), but if dopamine waves carry credit-assignment information (as Hamid argues), then the habitual system's *expression* will still respond to phasic DA even if its weights never see reward. The devaluation-insensitivity prediction (Stage 4) depends on the habitual system being blind to reward devaluation *in its value representation*. If the dopaCTRNN uses `W_eff = f(DA) · W` with a shared DA signal, then a tonic dopamine baseline driven by *reward availability* (part of the project's tonic baseline model, Section 2.3) could leak value information into the habitual expression gain. Hamid's framework suggests that this leakage can be prevented only if the tonic DA baseline is computed *outside* the reward path—e.g., purely from deprivation state or novelty—and the phasic/requested component is sourced *only from the goal-directed system's responsibility tag*, decoupled from the habitual system's actions.

4. **Emergent handoff requires dynamic allocation of credit, not just dopamine level.** Hamid shows that opponent waves redirect credit *within a single trial* in response to online evidence for agency (locomotion-sensory congruence). The dopaCTRNN's handoff mechanism (Stage 5) is currently modeled as a quasi-static tradeoff: the goal-directed DA-request falls, the tonic baseline is fixed, and habitual gain rises. Hamid suggests that a richer emergent transition might involve *dynamic reallocation* of where waves direct credit as the habitual system's competence rises—i.e., the postsynaptic targets of DA shift from DMS to DLS not just because overall DA level changes, but because the wave-like structures themselves reorganize (a "reorganization" the dopaCTRNN does not currently model, since it uses a scalar DA signal). Whether this level of detail is necessary for reproducing Villet's phenomena is an open question, but Hamid's evidence makes it explicit that credit routing is an active, task-dependent process, not a passive consequence of DA level alone.

## Open questions / caveats

- **Scalar vs. spatiotemporal dopamine in the dopaCTRNN.** The model currently uses a scalar DA signal because the task (T-maze) is spatially simple (one bit of working memory) and a scalar signal is computationally tractable for the attractor analysis pipeline. Hamid's data do not directly constrain whether a scalar signal *suffices* for the goal-directed/habitual transition—only that in a more complex task with richer spatial structure (Hamid's open-field running task), spatiotemporal heterogeneity emerges. A stage-specific question: does Stage 5 (emergent handoff) still occur if the DA signal is scalar? If yes, the model is robust to this simplification. If no, then the scalar assumption is not justified and the project must either adopt a spatiotemporal model (higher complexity, new analysis pipeline needed) or operationalize Hamid's credit-assignment findings differently (e.g., via learned asymmetries in the postsynaptic transfer function *f*).

- **MoE level 1 vs. goal-directed DA-request.** Hamid's level-1 expert computes evidence for *controllability*—a binary or graded judgment of whether actions cause rewards. The dopaCTRNN's goal-directed system solves the full working-memory task (which arm was sampled, do not match on test). These are not equivalent: a system can have poor knowledge of action-outcome contingency but excellent working-memory capacity. The project's DA-request mechanism (currently described as minimizing its own request, optimized by ordinary gradient descent) does not explicitly represent controllability or "responsibility." Should the DA-request neuron be trained to predict controllability (a level-1 analog) rather than simply minimizing? This would require a supervision signal (e.g., whether the current action sequence is actually controlling the reward), which re-raises the circularity concern flagged in Section 2.3 of the project overview.

- **Ramp slope as a sufficient statistic for credit allocation.** Hamid observes a negative correlation between DMS ramp slope and latency-to-peak DA at reward (r ≈ −0.4), and interprets this as the ramp encoding "responsibility." But the correlation is moderate and not observed in all conditions (notably, it is not significant in some passive/non-contingent contexts reported in the paper's SI). Does ramp slope alone *determine* credit allocation, or is it one of several signals? The dopaCTRNN's attempt to implement credit-allocation via DA-request would benefit from testing whether a single ramp signal suffices or whether multiple signals (ramp + wave direction + sub-expert RPEs) are necessary.

- **Generalization to different task structures.** Hamid's data are from a simple two-choice task (instrumental vs. Pavlovian) with a single state variable (progress to reward signaled by tone). The dopaCTRNN's T-maze task has richer structure (sample arm, delay, test arm choice). Does the MoE's hierarchical credit-assignment machinery (especially level 2 and 3) scale to this task, and do the same wave reversals and anticipatory ramps emerge? This is a question for Stage 6 (D1/D2 deepening) if wave-like structure is attempted, but for now remains a caveat about the ecological validity of Hamid's model to the specific target task.
