# traditionalvsnewdopa

_How the literature in this project repositions dopamine from a simple scalar signal to a complex, multi-dimensional neuromodulator._

## The classical view

The dominant twentieth-century account of phasic dopamine, crystallised by Schultz and colleagues and formalised through reinforcement learning, treats dopamine as a single global scalar reporting a **reward prediction error (RPE)**: the temporal-difference (TD) quantity `δ = r + γV(s') − V(s)`. Midbrain dopamine neurons fire above baseline when outcomes exceed prediction, pause when they fall short, and fall silent once a predictor is fully learned. This signal is then broadcast diffusely to striatum and cortex, where it gates synaptic plasticity, teaching downstream circuits which states and actions are valuable. The account is elegant and explanatory: it accommodates blocking, second-order conditioning, the gradual back-propagation of the error to the earliest reliable predictor, and the extinction of the response once prediction is perfect. Several papers in this vault retain this core as a tractable substrate — [[yaghoubi2026]] shows hippocampal CA1 reward codes shifting *backward* over weeks exactly as a TD error with discount `γ > 0.1` predicts, and [[lloyd_tamping_2015]] takes the TD-error theory as the baseline it must reconcile with newer data.

Its dominance came from doing real work with one number. A scalar RPE is enough to drive value learning across arbitrary tasks, requires no anatomical detail, and maps cleanly onto a normative computation. For a model builder it is also convenient: a single signal can be wired into any actor-critic loss. The dopaCTRNN project inherits this convenience for its goal-directed system, which is trained by ordinary RPE-driven actor-critic RL ([[Project overview a dopamine-mediated mechanism for the goal-directed-to-habitual handoff]], Stage 1).

## What the classical view cannot explain

The scalar-RPE account leaves several phenomena central to this project unaddressed. First, it has nothing to say about the **goal-directed-to-habitual transition** or its *reversibility*: [[villet2025]] shows behaviour shifting from devaluation-sensitive to devaluation-insensitive while the goal-directed (mPFC) trace remains dormant and *instantly* reactivatable when the habitual (DLS) system is silenced — a fallback a pure teaching-signal account cannot produce, since RPE explains what is learned, not how expression of an intact, learned circuit is gated on or off in milliseconds. Second, dopamine plainly affects *behaviour and cognition online*, not just learning: [[westbrook_striatal_2025]] dissociates dopamine's effect on working-memory reliance and effort-cost sensitivity from its effect on RL rates, and [[cools_2019]] catalogues drug effects that are diametrically opposite in the *same* individual across tasks — impossible if dopamine were a single error signal. Third, the same dopamine response can carry *opposite* meaning depending on cognitive framing ([[lloyd_reframing_2023]]), and a teaching signal need not be value-based at all ([[greenstreet_dopaminergic_2025]]). These are not edge cases; they are the phenomena the project exists to model.

## Dimension 1: spatial heterogeneity

The first crack in "uniform broadcast" is anatomical. [[hamid_wave-like_2021]] shows dopamine release across dorsal striatum is not globally synchronised but propagates as directional **spatiotemporal waves** (~0.1–1 mm/s), with opponent mediolateral vs. lateromedial trajectories on instrumental vs. Pavlovian trials. The *timing* of when each subregion receives dopamine implements graded credit assignment via eligibility-trace mismatch — DMS before DLS in instrumental trials sculpts asymmetric learning. [[frank_adaptive_2025]] and [[cools_2019]] reinforce this: dopamine effects depend on which frontostriatal loop is targeted, and there is a ventral-to-dorsal gradient in reuptake speed, so the same midbrain signal has different functional consequences depending on its receiving subregion. The dopaCTRNN deliberately simplifies this to a scalar (the one-bit T-maze is spatially trivial), but flags it as an acknowledged abstraction.

## Dimension 2: receptor-level complexity (D1/D2)

Even at a single site, "dopamine level" is not one variable. D1 and D2 receptors differ in affinity — D1 low-affinity, recruited by **phasic** bursts; D2 high-affinity, engaged by **tonic** baseline ([[frank_adaptive_2025]]; the project's phasic/tonic engine, overview §2.2) — and implement **opponent** pathways: [[jaskir_normative_nodate]] (OpAL*) shows D1 accumulating evidence for actions and D2 against, with a nonlinear Hebbian rule and dopamine acting as a fast multiplicative gain reweighting the two by environmental richness. [[kutter_dopamine_2026]] demonstrates at the single-neuron level that D1 and D2 differentially control not just the *strength* but the *temporal dynamics* of prefrontal decision codes — D1 contracting coding to a transient/dynamic regime, D2 sustaining persistent attractor-like activity (though the project cites Kutter narrowly, for *learnable, context-dependent* roles, not the affinity claim). Crucially, [[cools_2011]] and [[cools_2019]] establish the **inverted-U**: dopamine's effect on working memory and cognitive control is non-monotonic and baseline-dependent, with a D1-dominated state giving stable, high-energy-barrier attractors (distractor resistance) and a D2-dominated state giving low-energy, flexible switching. The mapping is not a fixed D1=stability/D2=flexibility law; it is a tunable position on the stability-flexibility tradeoff.

## Dimension 3: functional multiplicity (beyond RPE)

Dopamine teaches more than value. [[greenstreet_dopaminergic_2025]] shows tail-of-striatum dopamine encodes an **action prediction error (APE)** — a value-free, movement-locked, outcome-insensitive signal that reinforces stimulus-action associations regardless of reward, and that pairs with RPE to drive an automatic goal-directed-to-habitual transfer. [[lloyd_reframing_2023]] recasts dopamine as a **controlled controller**: a quantity the brain actively manipulates (via counterfactual baseline shifts) to resolve Pavlovian-instrumental conflict, supporting opposite behaviours with the same transient. [[lloyd_tamping_2015]] adds **vigour and gain**: ramping dopamine reflects a quasi-tonic discounted-value `(1−γ)⟨V(s')⟩` signal and a direct multiplicative gain on decision accumulation, not only a TD error. [[westbrook_striatal_2025]] adds **effort-cost regulation**, dissociable from learning. Dopamine is thus simultaneously a teacher (RPE, APE), a motivator (vigour), a gain controller, and a cost-benefit arbiter.

## Dimension 4: dopamine as expression gate (not just teacher)

The deepest reframing for this project is the **plasticity-vs-expression** distinction. [[naude_dopamine_2024]] separates dopamine's two roles cleanly: DA-plasticity *builds* latent attractors through slow synaptic strengthening, while DA-excitability *reveals* them through instantaneous modulation of excitability — intact, learned weights remain behaviourally silent until neuromodulation gates their expression. This is precisely the substrate the dopaCTRNN's `W_eff = f(DA) · W` design requires: weights `W` are learned by RL/APE and never touched by dopamine, while `f(DA)` sets how strongly they are expressed. It is the only account consistent with [[villet2025]]'s *instant* reactivation — decayed weights cannot snap back, but dopamine-suppressed intact weights can. [[jaskir_normative_nodate]] and [[frank_adaptive_2025]] independently model dopamine as a fast gain on already-learned weights rather than a slow rewriter, and [[lloyd_reframing_2023]] frames dopamine deployment as a controllable expression decision. Dopamine here is not (only) the teacher that writes the lesson, but the gate that decides whether the lesson is voiced.

## Synthesis: what this means for our model

The dopaCTRNN sits inside this updated, multi-dimensional picture and exploits some dimensions while deliberately simplifying others. It fully embraces **Dimension 4**: its central commitment, `W_eff = f(DA) · W`, treats dopamine as an expression gate on intact weights, the only design that reproduces [[villet2025]]'s instant fallback ([[Project overview a dopamine-mediated mechanism for the goal-directed-to-habitual handoff]], §2.1). It exploits **Dimension 3** by adopting [[greenstreet_dopaminergic_2025]]'s value-free APE as the habitual learning rule, making devaluation-insensitivity *structural* rather than incidental (§2.4), and by treating the goal-directed DA-*request* as a controllable, cost-minimised quantity in the spirit of [[lloyd_reframing_2023]] and [[westbrook_striatal_2025]] — the engine of an *emergent*, not scheduled, handoff (§2.3). It engages **Dimension 2** as an optional refinement: a phasic/tonic split through D1/D2 affinity is the multiple-timescale engine, with [[kutter_dopamine_2026]] grounding the prediction that D1 vs. D2 bias should reshape working-memory attractor geometry (more transient vs. more persistent), and the [[cools_2011]] inverted-U setting the expectation that the effect should be non-monotonic and baseline-dependent rather than a fixed role assignment. It **simplifies Dimension 1**: dopamine is a scalar, not a spatiotemporal wave, justified because the one-bit T-maze is spatially trivial — an abstraction the project explicitly flags against [[hamid_wave-like_2021]].

What the model contributes back is a concrete computational instantiation of the claim that prediction errors are *local* rather than a single global scalar — distinct RPE-trained and APE-trained subsystems whose interaction, gated by dopamine expression, produces the handoff as a learned consequence of redundancy rather than a hand-coded switch. If it works, it specifies a faster, biophysically grounded mechanism for the dual-system transfer that [[greenstreet_dopaminergic_2025]] demonstrates via slow weight decay; if it fails to produce an emergent handoff without an imposed low-rank constraint, that is itself an informative result about the minimal conditions for a Villet-style transition.
