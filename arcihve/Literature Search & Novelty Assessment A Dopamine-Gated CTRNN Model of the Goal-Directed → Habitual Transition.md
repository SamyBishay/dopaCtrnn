## TL;DR

- Your single most important piece of prior art is **Moskovitz, Miller, Sahani & Botvinick's MDL-C model** (arXiv:2211.07036, "A Unified Theory of Dual-Process Control," 2022; published as "Understanding dual process cognition via the minimum description length principle," _PLOS Computational Biology_ 20(10):e1012383, 18 Oct 2024): two concurrently-trained RNNs (a control policy and a default/habit policy) where the controller is penalized by a KL-divergence "cost of deviating from default," producing an _emergent_ handoff to the habit system that reverts to habit when the controller is ablated. The paper states verbatim that "behavior becomes less flexible as the duration of training increases. This shift is an emergent result of the MDL-C optimization objective." This overlaps heavily with your "self-minimizing DA-request → emergent handoff" claim and **must be cited and differentiated from**. Your novelty survives if you foreground (a) the explicit _request-for-a-costly-resource (dopamine)_ framing rather than information-theoretic policy compression, and (b) the ablation-**reactivation** falsification test, which appears genuinely unclaimed in the modeling literature.
- Your _individual_ mechanistic ingredients each have substantial prior art you are likely under-citing — Servan-Schreiber/Cohen gain theory and Eldar–Niv–Cohen neural gain (claim 1); Shenhav EVC, Keramati, Kool, Piray & Daw, Lieder & Griffiths (claim 2); Collins & Frank OpAL + Dreyer/Hunger affinity-kinetics models (claim 4); Watabe-Uchida/van Elzelingen/Gershman dopamine heterogeneity (claim 5); "Habits without Values" and OpAL (claim 6); Yin & Knowlton / Coutureau & Killcross / Packard & McGaugh (claims 3 & 7). The _specific combination_ — expression-gain separated from learning + self-minimizing DA request + ablation-reactivation test + D1/D2 affinity-based phasic/tonic split — does not appear to exist as a single model.
- The biggest citation gaps a committee will flag: **Servan-Schreiber, Printz & Cohen 1990** and **Eldar, Cohen & Niv 2013** (gain theory you build on but may not cite); **Shenhav, Botvinick & Cohen 2013** (EVC — the normative parent of your "minimize the request" idea); **Moskovitz et al./MDL-C 2024** and **Piray & Daw 2021** (closest computational competitors); **Coutureau & Killcross 2003** (the empirical reactivation result your falsification test models); and **Collins & Frank 2014 (OpAL)** plus a dopamine receptor-kinetics critique (**Hunger, Kumar & Schmidt 2020**) you should defend your D1/D2 split against.

## Key Findings

### Claim 1 — W_eff = f(DA)·W (gain on expression, not plasticity)

The core idea that a neuromodulator acts as a _multiplicative gain on an intact, already-learned network_ — separate from synaptic learning — is a mature, named theory you should cite explicitly:

- **Servan-Schreiber, Printz & Cohen (1990, _Science_ 249:892–895) "A network model of catecholamine effects: gain, signal-to-noise ratio, and behavior"** and **Cohen & Servan-Schreiber (1992; also 1993, _Schizophrenia Bulletin_)** are the foundational gain-modulation papers: catecholamines change the gain (slope) of units' activation functions, improving the network's signal-detection performance without changing weights. This is essentially your f(DA) applied to a transfer function rather than to W directly.
- **Eldar, Cohen & Niv (2013, _Nature Neuroscience_ 16:1146–1153) "The effects of neural gain on attention and learning"** and **Aston-Jones & Cohen (2005, _Annu. Rev. Neurosci._) adaptive gain theory** extend this to brain-wide gain and arousal — directly the literature you name.
- **"Gain modulation of recurrent networks" (Neurocomputing)** and **"Adaptive behavior with stable synapses" (arXiv:2404.07150, 2024)** show gain modulation specifically inside _recurrent_ networks with fixed weights — i.e. modulating expression of a competent network, which is your exact construct. The latter is explicit: gain modulation gives "adaptive behavior with stable synapses."
- **"Structured flexibility in recurrent neural networks via neuromodulation" (NM-RNN; bioRxiv 2024.07.26.605315)** is the closest machine-learning instantiation: a neuromodulatory subnetwork outputs a low-dimensional signal that _multiplicatively scales the low-rank recurrent weight matrix_ of an output-generating RNN — formally W_eff = g(t)·W, with the authors noting this "endows networks with gating mechanisms commonly found in artificial RNNs." Cite and differentiate (they use it for general gating/LSTM-like flexibility, not a DA-gated habit handoff).
- A standout you should know: **"Gain modulation of probabilistic selection without synaptic relearning" (_PMC_ 2024/2025)** explicitly argues gain "encodes memories of past experiences that change behavior without modification of synaptic efficacies" — the cleanest published statement of your expression-vs-weights separation.

**Assessment:** The W_eff = f(DA)·W _form_ is not novel in isolation; multiplicative neuromodulatory gain on a fixed/competent recurrent network is well-trodden (Servan-Schreiber 1990 → Eldar 2013 → NM-RNN 2024). Your novelty lies in _what the gain does functionally_ (mediating a goal-directed→habitual transition) and the D1/D2 split, not the gain mechanism per se. Present it that way.

### Claim 2 — Self-minimizing DA-request unit → emergent handoff

This is your most contested novelty claim, and there is a near-exact computational precursor:

- **Moskovitz, Miller, Sahani & Botvinick (arXiv:2211.07036, 2022; _PLOS Comput Biol_ 20(10):e1012383, 2024).** Two RNNs receive identical input: a control policy π and a default policy π₀. The objective is reward − λ[L(π₀)+L(π|π₀)], where L(π|π₀) is the KL divergence between control and default — literally a cost on how much the controller must intervene/deviate. As π₀ (the habit) is distilled toward competence, the controller's required deviation shrinks. They state the transition "is an emergent result of the MDL-C optimization objective," and ablating the control network makes behavior revert to the default/habit. **This is the single closest prior work to your self-minimizing-request → emergent-handoff mechanism.**
- **Piray & Daw (2021, _Nature Communications_ 12:4942) "Linear reinforcement learning in planning, grid fields, and cognitive control"** supplies the control-cost-as-KL-from-default formalism; learning the default policy yields "soft habits," with a larger control-cost weight λ producing stronger habitual bias.
- **Shenhav, Botvinick & Cohen (2013, _Neuron_ 79:217–240) Expected Value of Control** is the normative parent: control is intrinsically costly and agents "spontaneously seek to minimize it." **Kool, Gershman & Cushman (2017, _Psychological Science_) cost-benefit arbitration**, **Keramati, Dezfouli & Piray (2011, _PLOS Comput Biol_) speed/accuracy trade-off**, and **Lieder & Griffiths (2017, _Psych. Review_) strategy selection as rational metareasoning / Lieder, Shenhav, Musslick & Griffiths (2018, _PLOS Comput Biol_) "Rational metareasoning and the plasticity of cognitive control"** are the cost-of-control/metacontrol family you must cite.

**Assessment:** The _self-minimizing-request → emergent-handoff_ logic essentially exists in MDL-C, framed as policy compression (KL-to-default). Your distinct contributions are the explicit framing as a _request for a costly neuromodulatory resource (dopamine)_ emitted to a separate resource subsystem, and the biological dopamine grounding. Cite MDL-C, Piray & Daw, and EVC, and state your differentiation explicitly.

### Claim 3 — Ablation-reactivation as falsification of scheduled vs. emergent control

- **Empirical precedent is firmly established: Coutureau & Killcross (2003, _Behavioural Brain Research_ 146(1–2):167–174) "Inactivation of the infralimbic prefrontal cortex reinstates goal-directed responding in overtrained rats."** Verbatim: "lesions of the infralimbic prefrontal cortex cause animals to remain goal-directed even after substantial overtraining," and the study "demonstrates that the effects of overtraining on selective sensitivity to devaluation by specific satiety can be reversed following inactivation of the IL cortex." This — together with **Yin & Knowlton (2006, _Nat. Rev. Neurosci._ 7:464–476)**, **Yin, Knowlton & Balleine (2004, _Eur. J. Neurosci._; DLS lesions preserve goal-directed control)**, and **Smith & Graybiel (2012, _PNAS_; 2013, _Neuron_)** optogenetic IL inactivation reversing established habits — is the experimental result your in-silico test mirrors: silence the habit substrate → goal-directed control rises back up.
- **Computationally**, no model was found that uses the silence-the-habit → request-signal-rises logic as an explicit falsification of time/episode-dependent scheduling vs. competence-dependent arbitration. MDL-C ablates the _controller_ (opposite direction) and does not frame ablation as a scheduling-vs-competence test.

**Assessment:** Likely a genuine novel contribution at the modeling level, provided you cite Coutureau & Killcross 2003 and Yin & Knowlton 2006 as empirical grounding and acknowledge MDL-C's ablation experiment as the nearest computational neighbor (and note its opposite direction).

### Claim 4 — D1/D2 affinity-based phasic/tonic timescale split in a computational model

- **Collins & Frank (2014, _Psychological Review_ 121(3):337–366) OpAL** and **OpAL* (eLife 2023) "On the normative advantages of dopamine and striatal opponency"** are the canonical opponent D1/D2 actor models you already know via the Go/NoGo tradition — cite explicitly as your opponency parent. OpAL's two distinguishing features (opponent D1/D2 actors learning benefits vs. costs separately; nonlinear three-factor-Hebbian dynamics) map onto your two differently-tuned populations.
- For the _affinity-based phasic/tonic split as a multi-timescale mechanism_: **Dreyer, Herrik, Berg & Hounsgaard (2010, _J. Neurosci._ 30:14273)** (which you have) is the kinetics foundation — low-affinity D1 (EC50 ≈ 1 µM, bursts/phasic-preferring) vs. high-affinity D2 (EC50 ≈ 0.01 µM, tonic-preferring). **A mechanistic model of ADHD as dopamine phasic/tonic imbalance (Maith et al., _Front. Comput. Neurosci._ 2022)** builds a basal-ganglia model using D1/D2 receptor occupancy at different affinities.
- **CRITICAL CAVEAT YOU MUST ENGAGE: Hunger, Kumar & Schmidt (2020, _J. Neurosci._ 40(14):2868–2881) "Abundance Compensates Kinetics: Similar Effect of Dopamine Signals on D1 and D2 Receptor Populations"** (bioRxiv 2018) directly challenges the clean affinity-based split. Verbatim: "we found that the D1 and D2 dopamine receptor populations responded very similarly to tonic and phasic dopamine signals … because of slow unbinding rates, both receptor populations integrated dopamine signals over a timescale of minutes." (Note: authors are Hunger/Kumar/Schmidt, not "Marcott.")

**Assessment:** Using D1/D2 affinity to drive _two timescales of network behavior from one scalar DA signal_ in a CTRNN is a relatively under-explored, defensible niche. But a committee will expect you to address Hunger, Kumar & Schmidt 2020, which argues the affinity-based timescale split is not as clean as assumed — either rebut it or explicitly frame the split as a deliberate modeling idealization.

### Claim 5 — Local (non-global) prediction errors

- **Greenstreet et al. (2025, _Nature_ 643:1333–1342) "Dopaminergic action prediction errors serve as a value-free teaching signal"** (your APE source) is confirmed: APE in tail-of-striatum dopamine, value-free, a distinct circuit from RPE, with the two prediction-error types "work[ing] in tandem to support learning, each reinforcing different types of association in different striatal areas" — exactly the "local, not global scalar" idea.
- Strong supporting literature: **Menegas et al. (2017, eLife); Parker et al. (2016, _Nat. Neurosci._); van Elzelingen, Goedhoop, Warnaar, Denys, Arbab & Willuhn (2022, _PNAS_ 119(21):e2117270119) "A unidirectional but not uniform striatal landscape of dopamine signaling"** — verbatim: "dopamine release is, regionally, extremely heterogeneous and … a reward prediction error–like signal is predominantly found in the relatively small limbic domain of the striatum"; **Berke (2018, _Nat. Neurosci._) "What does dopamine mean?"; Gershman et al. (2024) "Explaining dopamine through prediction errors and beyond"** (reviews dopamine "ensembles"/feature-specific channels vs. a single scalar RPE).

**Assessment:** Well-supported; the non-uniform/heterogeneous-dopamine literature is precisely your foundation. Add van Elzelingen 2022 and Gershman 2024 to anchor "not a single global scalar."

### Claim 6 — RNN/CTRNN model of goal-directed → habitual transition

- **Miller, Shenhav & Ludvig (2019, _Psychological Review_) "Habits without Values"** is the key value-free computational habit model (devaluation/contingency insensitivity emerge from direct action-strengthening rather than outcome encoding) — pairs naturally with Greenstreet APE. Cite as your habit-rule parent.
- **Dezfouli & Balleine (2012, 2013) habits-as-action-sequences/chunking** models (the hierarchical/sequence view of habit).
- **Keramati et al. (2011)** and **Keramati, Smittenaar, Dolan & Dayan (2016, _PNAS_) "plan-until-habit spectrum"** are the dual-system transition models.
- **MDL-C (Moskovitz 2024)** is the closest _RNN-based_ dual-process transition model (again, cite/differentiate).

**Assessment:** No existing RNN/CTRNN model combines dopamine-gated expression + a value-free habit rule + emergent handoff to model the devaluation-sensitivity-loss transition the way you describe. MDL-C is RNN-based but lacks dopamine grounding and the D1/D2 split; "Habits without Values" and Keramati are not recurrent-network models. This is your strongest architecture-level novelty footing.

### Claim 7 — Allocentric (goal-directed) vs. egocentric (habitual) observation asymmetry

- Strong support: **Packard & McGaugh (1996)** double dissociation (hippocampal/allocentric vs. caudate/egocentric in the plus-maze); **McDonald & White (1994) "Parallel information processing in the water maze"**; **Yin & Knowlton (2004, 2006)**; **Fouquet et al. (2013, _PLOS One_)** complementary hippocampus/DMS roles in allocentric vs. sequence-based navigation. The asymmetry — early goal-directed lesions block learning while late habitual lesions don't — maps onto the DMS-vs-DLS lesion literature (DMS lesions prevent acquisition/force habit; DLS lesions preserve goal-directed control even after overtraining).

**Assessment:** Your modeling assumption is well-grounded empirically; cite Packard & McGaugh 1996 and McDonald & White 1994 as the dissociation foundation, with Yin & Knowlton for the instrumental/striatal version.

## Details: Does your specific combination exist?

No single paper combines all four signature mechanisms. The closest single competitor is **MDL-C (Moskovitz et al. 2022/2024)**, which independently realizes (i) two concurrently-trained RNNs for control vs. habit, (ii) a cost penalizing the controller's intervention, (iii) emergent (not scheduled) handoff, and (iv) controller-ablation reverting to habit. Your model is distinguished by: dopamine as the gated resource with W_eff = f(DA)·W expression-gain; the D1/D2 affinity-based phasic/tonic split; the APE value-free habit rule; and the ablation-**reactivation** (silence-the-habit) falsification direction. Each is individually defensible, but the thesis should be explicit that the _architecture-level_ novelty is the integration plus the reactivation test, not any single ingredient. Treat MDL-C, Piray & Daw, EVC, OpAL, and the Servan-Schreiber/Eldar gain lineage as the five reference points your "Related Work" must explicitly position against.

4. **Defend claim 4 against Hunger, Kumar & Schmidt 2020 ("Abundance compensates kinetics," _J. Neurosci._ 40:2868).** Either cite and rebut it (e.g., on grounds of downstream MSN integration, plasticity thresholds, or your timescale regime) or explicitly soften the affinity-based split to a modeling idealization.
